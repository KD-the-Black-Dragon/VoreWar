# Design clarifications after architecture packet 01C

**Status:** User decisions recorded on 2026-10-30. Planning only; no gameplay, source, scene, prefab, or asset changes. These decisions refine the [campaign plan](mercenary-company-campaign-plan.md) and affected specs. All follow-up outcome questions below are resolved; remaining numeric examples are not final tuning.

## Decisions, using the original question numbers

1. **Resurrection and replacement traits:** Permanently deprecate existing revival, reincarnation, saved-copy, and replacement effects that circumvent permanent loss. The user has no interest in bringing these back. The company leader's optional paid revival cutscene is the only intended revival. Physical file retirement remains subject to repository rules. Follow-up confirmed that completed digestion, not downing, triggers leader revival/game over; the leader alone has this unique immortality.
2. **Downed:** Replace every surrendered state/presentation with downed. A downed unit cannot receive or execute commands for the rest of the battle. Reaching 0 HP always causes downed unless an actual digestion/final-loss outcome takes precedence. Fear/pain/surrender effects may also down a unit above 0 HP. Healing must not silently restore its ability to act. The previously retired voluntary surrender button stays retired.
3. **Targeting downed units:** Ordinary attacks must not deliberately target them. Collateral area damage may still reach them but cannot permanently kill them; they remain downed. They have a 100% chance of being successfully consumed; this does not promise immediate digestion or replace other retained digestion rules. Digestion is the only means of permanently removing an otherwise persistent character. Summons disappear rather than becoming downed.
4. **Battle completion:** Downed and contained living units still count as present. The intended normal rule is to continue until every unit on a side is dead, fled, or digested. If only downed company members remain while standing enemies remain, continue simulating the battle through actual consumption/digestion; retreating active members does not create an assumed casualty result. A stalemate applies when both sides have zero active units, or when the battle cannot finish even after an excessively generous turn limit. All remaining units then count as fled and survive. Two hundred turns is an example, not a final tuned limit. Do not wake downed units, force permanent deaths, or assume off-screen digestion to break a stall.
5. **Enemy cleanup:** Downed enemies do not automatically become a victorious results screen or prisoners. Preserve the existing simulated/automatic battle resolution control, with AI prioritizing consumption of downed opponents to finish the battle. Do not add a new prisoner-management system.
6. **Enemy retreat with captives:** Enemies must not flee carrying swallowed captives. They must release them first or be unable to flee, including during a rout. Audit ordinary, AI, forced, and special retreat paths. Whether player retreat needs the same restriction is not decided by this answer.
7. **Combat healing:** Spell healing may remain as temporary HP removed after combat. Healing from vore remains lasting. Follow-up explicitly chose separate lasting/temporary pools over a literal lowest-HP record, so lasting vore healing must not be undone at battle end.
8. **Temporary HP and automatic recovery:** Use distinguishable lasting and temporary HP, with temporary HP shown separately (grey is the example), no passive time decay, and removal at battle end. Damage spends temporary HP first: 50 lasting + 30 temporary taking 20 damage ends combat at 50 lasting after the remaining temporary HP clears. Spells and traits can provide temporary healing; vore healing raises lasting HP. Ordinary permanent recovery comes from elapsed time, rest, or vore, rather than automatic end-of-battle full heals. Leader revival and the explicit low-HP post-battle baseline in answer 18 are separate exceptions. Pool caps and injury/deep-attrition interaction still need refinement before implementation.
9. **Injury sources:** Many damage sources, including digestion, poison, magic, and environmental damage, can have small injury chances. Do not restrict the system to weapon attacks. Distinguish damage from health costs/initialization, avoid duplicate rolls for one hit, and preserve the already agreed hit/deep-attrition checks.
10. **Summons:** The generic creature-summoning spell should select animals only. Temporary sapient clones, doubles, or illusions are acceptable; they do not become permanent company members. Earlier documentation's blanket animal-only interpretation was too restrictive. Species curation and enablement still apply.
11. **Permanent species changes:** Deprecate the optional conversion mechanic the user excluded and any polymorph spell that would permanently turn a company member into an animal. This does not by itself retire every temporary disguise or copy mechanic.
12. **Allegiance:** No mid-combat conversions or spontaneous allegiance switches. Handcrafted future rescue/hiring events may offer a recruit with a quoted salary and an explicit player choice; towns need not be the only possible hiring source forever. No such new event is required for MVP.
13. **Species disabling:** Follow-up confirmed that disabling a species blocks newly generated individuals while preserving every existing individual: leader, current members, offered candidates, former members, and existing contract enemies. No campaign-start lock or retroactive removal. Reappearance/rehire of a persistent individual is not new generation. This refines earlier wording that disabled species would never be seen at all. Source findings are below.
14. **Leader distinction:** Deprecate Leadership as well as the aura, including its automatic growth and obsolete power terms. Make the leader personally tougher through starting stats and/or extra stat growth rather than a company-wide stat buff. Keep the shared equipment and leveling framework; exact bonuses are tuning decisions.
15. **Loadout:** Nine slots: helmet, torso armor, leg armor, arm armor, one accessory (ring/amulet), melee weapon, ranged weapon, and two spells. Do not reinterpret these as nine interchangeable legacy item slots.
16. **Attacks:** Keep the existing melee/ranged action keys (1 and 2). Melee uses the equipped melee weapon automatically, even if the unit also has a ranged weapon; use natural/unarmed attacks when appropriate without a melee weapon. No new weapon-selection interface is requested.
17. **Appearance:** Visible armor changes are not a current goal. Mechanical armor slots and item display are sufficient initially.
18. **HP-changing equipment and post-battle baseline:** Equipping maximum-HP gear raises current and maximum HP together; removing it lowers both together. Keep the adjustment symmetric rather than awarding repeated free heals. Critically injured survivors should be raised to a modest post-battle HP baseline; 10% or 20% are illustrative and no exact percentage is approved. Resolve temporary-HP removal before determining the final baseline; do not apply it to permanently lost units or as a way to revive the dead. Safe unequip behavior near 0 HP needs review before the equipment batch.
19. **Loot:** Ordinary battlefield gear loot remains undecided for balance. The user leans toward a digested member's equipment being destroyed with them. Do not implement guaranteed gear salvage, enemy drops, or a blanket no-loot rule from this answer. Collateral damage is confirmed nonfatal. Follow-up explicitly supersedes earlier abandonment losses: there is no left-behind state or assumed digestion.
20. **Retrying a target:** Casualties remain gone; surviving enemies retain their state and should recover HP at a typical daily rate. Represent the contract force as an army at its map location, using the appropriate reusable healing rules. This does not restore roaming strategic enemies, empire turns, replacement casualties, or a re-roll to the original power budget. Exact recovery rate is tuning work.
21. **Normal saves:** Saving inside battles is desired, including the state needed to resume correctly. Between-battle saving is an acceptable initial fallback if battle persistence is technically problematic; report any fallback rather than silently weakening support.
22. **Ironman:** Aim to save every turn with several corruption-recovery backups; choose the nearest practical behavior if technical limits prevent this. Packet 01D must assess both campaign days and tactical turns, containment, temporary HP, downed state, and once-only results. Backup count and checkpoint boundaries are not yet implemented or proven feasible.
23. **Rest interruptions:** Multi-day rest must pause for missed-payday warnings and other decisions requiring a response, preserving the opportunity to pay before departures. Do not skip directly through the warning's grace day.
24. **Insufficient payday funds:** If the company cannot pay everyone, record each unpaid wage as personal debt without automatically choosing recipients. Show current gold, total debt, shortfall, and any members threatening to leave. Offer: (a) pay the displayed total for all members threatening to leave, enabled only if affordable; (b) View Company for individual full-debt payments; (c) OK to return to the map without payment. The group payment is a player choice, not automatic prioritization. Existing debt persists and individual payments remain all-or-nothing.

## Current species controls: source findings

The user's impression is broadly correct for the **mercenary eligibility toggles**, but not for every control on the legacy combined page:

- [MercenaryHouse.UpdateStaticStock/UpdateStock](../Assets/Scripts/Entities/MercenaryHouse.cs) uses `Merc {race}` to select future candidates. [Village.UpdateMercenaries](../Assets/Scripts/Entities/Village.cs) does likewise. These inspected paths do not remove already hired company/army units simply because their species toggle becomes false. Candidate stock also refreshes through its ordinary replacement logic; this is not a universal guarantee that all existing offers remain forever.
- [ContentSettings.SetValues](../Assets/Scripts/Subwindows/ContentSettings.cs#L1144) explicitly replaces a matching empire's army list with an empty list when that monster spawner is disabled and the world/empire exists. Thus disabling a monster spawner can discard its existing armies, not just prevent future generation. Packet 02A moved preference loading out of this component; this older apply side effect is still present pending later retirement.
- [The summon spell](../Assets/Scripts/Entities/Spells/SpellList.cs#L939) can consult either mercenary or spawner enablement, while other setup, copy, and mapping paths have separate behavior. There is no single legacy species-availability guarantee to inherit; see [packet 01B](architecture/01b-species-events-and-narrative.md).

This is source inspection, not a runtime test. The replacement page must not copy the old army-clearing side effect. The user has now confirmed preservation of all existing individuals, including offers, former members, and contract enemies.

## Follow-up decisions

- **Leader trigger resolved:** Merely downed leaders remain alive and receive the normal survivor HP baseline. Only completed digestion triggers enabled revival, or immediate game over if revival is disabled. With enabled revival, the existing survivors/reserves requirement and results-before-cutscene sequence remain. Summons disappear when they would become downed; they have no persistent death/revival record.
- **Collateral damage resolved:** It cannot permanently kill a downed persistent character.
- **HP model resolved:** Separate pools, temporary HP spent first, lasting vore healing retained.
- **Stalemate resolved:** Both sides have zero active units, or the fight remains unable to finish after an excessively generous turn limit (200 turns is illustrative). Declare stalemate and treat everyone remaining as fled; everyone goes home. This includes surviving downed units. Apply the enemy captive-release restriction before treating enemies as fled; do not silently lose living captives off-map. The final numeric limit remains tuning work.
- **Species policy resolved:** Block new individuals, preserve all existing ones; no forced campaign-start lock.
- **No abandonment state:** This explicitly supersedes all earlier left-behind/permanent-abandonment-loss statements. When standing enemies remain against downed company members, the battle must play out through actual outcomes. The player can delegate the remainder to the existing AI simulation; it must still simulate, not assume deaths. A stalemate instead returns remaining company members as survivors, not memorial entries. No rescue/recoverability subsystem is implied.
- **Draw contracts resolved:** The normal fleeing-enemy rule applies even on a draw: fled enemies leave the game and count as defeated. Do not retain a retryable enemy force solely because the result is a stalemate. When all target members are defeated through digestion/death/flight, including stalemate flight, complete the contract and grant its normal automatic reward/reputation once, even though the battle result is labelled a draw.

These follow-up questions are resolved. The user subsequently authorized resuming the study; [packet 01D](architecture/01d-campaign-persistence-and-identity.md) and the consolidated cleanup order are now prepared for review. Recording these answers did not authorize gameplay implementation.
