## Goals and boundaries

- Derive provisional values from explicit mathematics and reproducible native simulations. The user supplies design goals rather than guessing coefficients or conducting extensive balance playtests.
- Start economy tuning around a leader and two hired mercenaries. The leader still has no personal wage, but the company now requires baseline weekly upkeep even with no employees. A successful entry-level contract should fund one basic replacement hire, including its equipment and prorated advance wages, plus a surplus **after recurring wages and recovery**. Include the new company upkeep when calculating recurring costs; report the allocation explicitly.
- Basic armor should be inexpensive and modestly useful. Better armor should be a meaningful, increasingly expensive investment in an individual. Digestion destroys a lost mercenary's worn gear; replacement accounting must include that loss. Do not assume the player equips every member equally.
- Weekly wages should rise slowly at first and increasingly quickly at higher levels. A company accepting sufficiently high levels must eventually lose money farming contracts far below its capabilities. Before a player commits a level-up, show the individual's old and new weekly wage and the resulting company weekly total. Deferring a level-up must remain possible.
- The leader requires baseline upkeep covering the necessities of operating the company, including food, commodities, licenses and guild memberships. It is represented through the leader in company management. A positive starting amount increases by a fixed amount each week, creating an unavoidable economic clock through deferred levels, rest and idling. Opening pressure should be meaningful and become relatively less important beside later income and wages. The company treasury supports the leader's livelihood and business. Personal leader wages remain zero.
- Preserve the approved AC dice roll, defense order/minimum damage, equipment evasion coverage, four stats, AP/action rules, vore/digestion/fates, leader revival, injury rules and HP recovery at 10%/20%/30%. Numerical calibration is distinct from changing those mechanics.
- Keep fixed contract difficulty independent of company strength. Power estimates guide generation and comparisons; prices do not define combat power, and a power score is not proof of fair outcomes.
- No new narrative, post-battle event system, Help prose rewrite, species, item lore or release/publishing work. Crystaline/Fractured remains explicitly deferred.
- Reuse the existing Battle Lab, native combat and CompanyBalanceAudit. Any later diagnostic extensions require normal source notices/CHANGES and Unity-compatible code. ### Confirmed payday and insolvency rules — 2026-11-05

1. Leader upkeep is due weekly on the regular payday schedule and must be processed before mercenary wages. Track its outstanding balance and insolvency deadline separately from individual mercenary debts. Display the obligation against the leader, reflecting the basic necessities of the leader and company.
2. If company upkeep debt from the preceding payday remains when the next payday arrives, the campaign ends for critical insolvency. The user must pay it **before** that payday; do not silently grant another grace period or process new wages first. Preserve this deadline through rest, save/load, bankruptcy notices and other interrupted daily decisions.
3. Otherwise, process the new upkeep bill first. Automatically spend available gold up to the amount due. If gold is insufficient, the unpaid shortfall becomes upkeep debt. Notify the player that the company is in critical danger of insolvency, state the upkeep debt and next-payday deadline, and allow the grace week. With the treasury exhausted, employee wages become their respective personal debts under the existing wage rules.
4. Process ordinary employee wages only after the upkeep allocation. Preserve individual wage debts, player-owned repayment choices and employee departure rules; no assumed player choice among unpaid mercenaries. Employee debt alone is not this new critical-insolvency trigger.
5. A mercenary's accepted level-up changes the wage charged at the **next regular payday**. No immediate midweek top-up and no retroactive increase to existing debt or an advance already paid.
6. During the grace week, provide an explicit **Pay Upkeep** control associated with the leader. Follow the existing wage-debt payment interaction. The player directs repayment from company gold, and the outstanding upkeep balance/deadline remain visible until cleared.

Provide clear upkeep, outstanding upkeep debt and deadline information alongside the existing wage/payday information. A rest sequence must surface the warning and permit a response before the deadline passes. Validate exact payday boundaries, zero employees, partial automatic payment on payday, manual debt repayment, insufficient upkeep plus wages, debt cleared before the deadline, debt outstanding at the deadline despite cash in the treasury, interrupted daily processing, and resumed saves. Use the normal campaign-ending and Ironman handling for this new loss cause. Synthetic checks must suppress actual deletion and retain their files. Establish an explicit upgrade policy before enabling upkeep in old campaigns so a new field or old campaign age cannot accidentally create retroactive fatal debt.
